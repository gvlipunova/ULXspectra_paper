\section{Radiative transfer in the wind funnel: Monte Carlo}
\label{app:mc}

\subsection{What the ray cast assumes}
\label{app:mc:raycast}

The emitting surface of the wind is the surface $\taup = 1$ (Sect.~\ref{sec:wind:photosphere}), that is the last-scattering surface for photons that leave perpendicular to the disc.
In the ray cast of Sect.~\ref{sec:spectrum} every element of this surface radiates a blackbody, Lambertian about its normal, and the wind above the surface is transparent to the observer.
% source: docs/green_surface_monte_carlo.md, "What the code does"
The temperatures are normalised so that the emitted energy is right (Sect.~\ref{sec:wtemp:budget}).
The light that the surface itself blocks on the way to the observer is dropped, not redistributed, so that the energy received by observers at all inclinations, $\int L_{\rm iso}\,{\rm d}\mu$, is below the emitted energy.
For the three fits of this appendix the share of the emitted energy that reaches observers is 0.86 (\srcHoIX), 0.95 (\srcNGCthirteen) and 0.50 (\srcNGCfiftyfour).
% source: docs/green_surface_monte_carlo.md, table "share of emitted energy that reaches observers"; commit 4da7c04
% Source names: macros in main.tex (author 2026-11-01: full names; NGC1313B to be decided)

Seen obliquely, the surface of the last scattering lies elsewhere.
In a plane-parallel scattering atmosphere the surface $\tau = 1$ along a ray at the cosine $\mu$ to the normal lies at $\taup = \mu$, slightly above the surface $\taup = 1$.
This is limb darkening: for pure electron scattering \citep{Chandrasekhar1960}
\begin{equation}
\frac{I(\mu)}{I_{\rm Lambert}} = \frac{1 + 2.06\,\mu}{2.373} ,
\label{eq:mc_limb}
\end{equation}
% source: docs/green_surface_monte_carlo.md, "Analytic expectations" (Chandrasekhar)
which is 1.29 at $\mu = 1$, 0.86 at $\mu = 0.5$ and 0.42 at $\mu \to 0$.
The Lambertian elements of the code give the same total, but miss this redistribution over angles by 30--60\%.
In an extended wind with $\rho \propto R^{-2}$, $R$ the spherical radius, the optical depth from the distance $R$ to infinity along a straight ray at the angle $\psi$ to the radius vector is $\tau = \tau_R\,\psi/\sin\psi$, where $\tau_R = \kT\rho R$ is the radial optical depth.
% derived (Claude, 2026-11-01): impact parameter b = r sin(psi); int_{r cos psi}^inf ds/(b^2 + s^2) = psi/b,
%         so tau = kappa rho r^2 psi/b = tau_r psi/sin(psi)
Since $\tau_R \propto 1/R$, the photosphere seen along rays leaving at $\psi$ lies at $\psi/\sin\psi$ times the radius of the radial one, that is further out by at most a factor $\pi/2$ for the rays that point outwards, $\psi \le \pi/2$.
% source: docs/green_surface_monte_carlo.md, "Analytic expectations" (factor psi/sin psi <= pi/2); psi defined by Claude (author 2026-11-01: define)
Neither estimate covers the case that dominates in our fits.
Where $\taup < 1$ through the whole wind, the surface $\taup = 1$ lies on the disc, yet an inclined ray crosses the wind cone over a long path, and the optical depth along it is $\gg 1$.

\subsection{Method}
\label{app:mc:method}

We followed photon packets with a Monte Carlo code (\code{docs/scripts/mc\_green\_surface.py} in the code repository).
The packets start on the emitting surface of the model, with the temperatures of the model: the wind photosphere and the directly seen disc inside $\rout$.
They are drawn in proportion to $T^4\,{\rm d}A$ and emitted Lambertian about the local normal, as in the ray cast.
% source: docs/green_surface_monte_carlo.md, "Monte Carlo"; mc_green_surface.py docstring
We considered two cases, which differ only in what happens to a packet after it leaves the surface.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Scattering:} the packets scatter on electrons in the wind density of the model (Sect.~\ref{sec:wind:density}, with the ballistic speed law) until they escape.
Scattering is Thomson, isotropic and conservative, without change of photon energy, and the disc plane reflects diffusely with the albedo of unity.
This is the physical case within these assumptions.
\item \emph{Reflection:} the wind has no opacity, and the surface $\taup = 1$ is an opaque diffuse mirror.
This is the geometry of the ray cast, but with the blocked light kept.
\end{itemize}
% source: docs/green_surface_monte_carlo.md, "Monte Carlo" and the table "How to read the lines"
The ratio of the reflection case to the ray cast thus gives the effect of dropping the occulted light.
The ratio of the scattering case to the reflection case gives the effect of the wind above the surface $\taup = 1$, that is of defining the emitting surface by $\taup$.
The ray cast of the code is reproduced in the script; at the best-fit inclination it gives the bolometric $L_{\rm iso}$ within 1.5\% of the code.
% source: docs/green_surface_monte_carlo.md, "Monte Carlo" (Lbol_iso within 1.5% of model_energy_report.txt)

We used the best fits of \srcHoIX, \srcNGCthirteen and \srcNGCfiftyfour with the preset BH3a over the full energy range, with $4\times10^4$ packets per run (Table~\ref{tab:mc_fits}).
All three have $\theta_{\rm c} = 45.6^\circ$ ($\omf = 0.7$) and $\theta_{\rm d} = 90^\circ$, so that the edge of the funnel is at $i \approx 46^\circ$.
% source: docs/green_surface_monte_carlo.md, "Monte Carlo" (fits, packets, theta_c, theta_d)
The preset BH3a is the main model (Sect.~\ref{sec:model}) for a BH with the rule \code{Ledd} for $\Rsph$: the disc beyond $\Rsph$ releases $\LEdd$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:rsph_Ledd}.
Its free parameters are $M$, $\mdot_0$, $f_{\rm in}$, $r_{\rm in,f}$, $\rout$, $i$, $\epsilon_{\rm w}$ and $\beta_{\rm w}$; $\omf = 0.7$ and $\hd = 0$ are fixed (Table~\ref{tab:params}).
The full energy range means the whole SED from $10^{-4}$ to 100~keV, of which only 0.3--10~keV is constrained by X-ray data (Sect.~\ref{sec:fitting:data}).

\subsection{Spectra and luminosity against inclination}
\label{app:mc:spectra}

Figure~\ref{fig:mc_spectra} compares the intrinsic spectra, without absorption, of the ray cast and of the two Monte Carlo cases, and Table~\ref{tab:mc_ratio} gives the bolometric ratios.
The result depends on whether the line of sight lies inside the funnel, $i < \theta_{\rm c}$, or crosses the wind cone, $i > \theta_{\rm c}$.

Inside the funnel the ray cast underestimates $L_{\rm iso}$ by a factor of 1.5--2.4, and the shape of the spectrum above about 0.1~keV hardly changes.
For \srcHoIX at $9^\circ$ and \srcNGCthirteen at $19^\circ$ the ratio is flat to within about 10\% from 0.3 to 30~keV.
The factor has two parts.
The occulted light that the ray cast drops contributes a factor of 1.05--1.15 for \srcHoIX and \srcNGCthirteen and of 2.0 for the wind-dominated \srcNGCfiftyfour.
The rest, a factor of 1.2--1.8, is the collimation by the funnel: light scattered off the funnel walls leaves along the axis (Sect.~\ref{app:mc:funnel}).
% source: docs/green_surface_monte_carlo.md, "Conclusions" item 2; commit 4da7c04

Beyond the edge of the funnel the error is large.
The line of sight crosses the wind cone, which is thin perpendicularly but thick obliquely.
The ray cast treats the wind above the surface $\taup = 1$ as transparent and still sees the hot inner disc.
For \srcHoIX and \srcNGCthirteen it overestimates $L_{\rm iso}$ by a factor of 2.5--8 at $50$--$63^\circ$ (\srcNGCthirteen up to $75^\circ$), and its spectrum is too hard: the ratio of the Monte Carlo to the ray cast falls from 0.3--0.7 below 0.1~keV to 0.1--0.3 at 10~keV.
Close to the disc plane the ray cast gives no hard flux at all, while the scattered light keeps a hard tail.
The ratios above unity at the largest inclinations in Table~\ref{tab:mc_ratio} (\srcHoIX at $70^\circ$, \srcNGCfiftyfour at $63$--$70^\circ$) do not mean that the Monte Carlo is bright there.
They arise because the ray cast becomes dark: the outer part of the emitting surface hides the inner disc from the observer.
In \srcHoIX the wind photosphere rises to $z/r = 0.39$ at $x \approx 490$, which hides the surface well inside this radius for $i \gtrsim 68.5^\circ$, and $L_{\rm iso}/L_{\rm total}$ of the ray cast drops from 0.90 at $63^\circ$ to 0.033 at $70^\circ$.
In \srcNGCfiftyfour the photosphere reaches $z/r = 0.88$ at $x \approx 320$, so the hiding begins at $i \approx 49^\circ$, and $L_{\rm iso}/L_{\rm total}$ of the ray cast falls from 0.19 at $57^\circ$ to 0.015 at $70^\circ$.
The Monte Carlo, in contrast, changes smoothly with $i$: the light that leaks out of the funnel through the wind gives $L_{\rm iso}/L_{\rm total} \approx 0.06$--$0.09$ in both cases, and the reflection case keeps the light that the surface blocks.
In \srcNGCthirteen the photosphere is lower, $z/r \le 0.24$, the hiding begins only at $i \approx 77^\circ$, and the ratio stays below unity up to $75^\circ$.

%   MC scatter 0.130, 0.075, 0.061. Not in the doc.
% source: docs/green_surface_monte_carlo.md, "Conclusions" item 1; commit 4da7c04
The surface $\taup = 1$ is therefore a good emitting surface for oblique views only where the wind is optically thick perpendicularly.
Where it is thin perpendicularly, which is most of the surface in \srcHoIX and \srcNGCthirteen, the surface seen obliquely is a different one, the wind cone itself.
% source: docs/green_surface_monte_carlo.md, "Conclusions" item 3

The best-fit inclination of \srcNGCfiftyfour, $48.5^\circ$, lies just beyond the edge of the funnel, where both the ray cast and the Monte Carlo change fastest with $i$.
In the bin $45$--$52^\circ$ the bolometric ratio is 0.91, because the dropped light and the hidden centre nearly cancel, and the spectrum above 3~keV is about 30\% softer in the Monte Carlo.
This agreement is a coincidence, and the posterior of $i$ near the edge of the funnel is not reliable.
% source: docs/green_surface_monte_carlo.md, "Conclusions" item 5

The total emitted energy does not depend on the choice of the emitting surface, because it is fixed by $f_{\rm cor}$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:fcor}), and the temperatures depend on it only weakly, $T \propto A^{-1/4}$, by at most 25\%.
What an observer at a given $i$ receives depends on this choice strongly, through the projected area, the occultation and the dropped light.
The fitted parameters absorb this error.
% source: docs/green_surface_monte_carlo.md, "Conclusions" item 4

\subsection{Limits of the test}
\label{app:mc:limits}

These first results are preliminary.
The scattering is grey: isotropic Thomson scattering, without the Rayleigh phase function, without Doppler shifts or bulk Comptonization by the outflow, and without thermal Comptonization.
There is no absorption: a packet keeps the energy of the element that emitted it, and there is no thermalisation below the surface.
The disc plane has the albedo of unity.
The packets start on the surfaces of the model, so the test covers the transfer after emission, not where the light is emitted.
With $4\times10^4$ packets per run, a few hundred packets fall into each bin at $i \ge 70^\circ$.
% source: docs/green_surface_monte_carlo.md, "Limits of the Monte Carlo"
Only the BH fits were tested here; the NS central emitter was added to the Monte Carlo later (Sect.~\ref{app:mc:heldout}).
% source: docs/green_surface_monte_carlo.md, "Limits" (BH fits only); ULXspectra-mc commit ecdbb64 (NS central emitter)

\paragraph{The revised table.}
The results of this paper were obtained with the table described above, built from 117 runs.
We have since rebuilt the table in two steps.
First, its Monte Carlo runs were redone with the current model (the wind toll restricted to $r < \Rsph$ and the diluted wind spectrum), 28 runs with weak winds were added at all funnel openings, the bins in optical depth were refined to $\taup = 0$, 0.3, 1, 3, 6, 12, 25, 50 and 100, and a fourth ring class was added with a boundary at $\Rsph$ (145 runs).
This version left thick winds seen beyond the funnel edge at $i > 64^\circ$ too bright, by up to a factor of 1.5.
Second, each ring class was split into four sub-classes by the ring's place in its own wind: its optical depth relative to the largest one of the wind photosphere, and its radius relative to the outer edge of the photosphere, each above or below 0.3; 45 runs at the best fits of all BH presets and sources were added (190 runs).
For 17 held-out runs, the revised table gives MC/model per angle bin of 0.93--1.04 inside the funnel and 0.76--1.14 beyond its edge (117-run table: 0.64--1.22 beyond the edge; without the correction 0.59--2.49 inside and 0.37--18 beyond).
The typical error per angle bin beyond the edge falls from 12.6\% to 7.5\% for the held-out runs, from 14.4\% to 6.8\% for the best fits, and from 14.2\% to 9.9\% for BH4a fits between the nodes of the funnel angle; the excess of thick winds at $i > 64^\circ$ is gone.
All fits are being redone with the revised table.

\subsection{Held-out checks of the correction}
\label{app:mc:heldout}

We checked the correction against Monte Carlo runs that are not in the table: 17 BH runs (best fits of 7 sources and 5 presets, three of them at other $\mdot_0$) and the 14 NS best fits of NGC~5408, NGC~300 and NGC~55.
The NS fits here are earlier fits with the presets NS4a--NS7, in which two radius parameters are free (Sect.~\ref{sec:model:params}); the check uses only their best-fit models, so the implied prior on $\rmag$ does not affect it.
% source: PLAN C23 (old NS4a-NS7 fits superseded; quote them with the note on the implied prior)
Tables~\ref{tab:mc_heldout_bh} and~\ref{tab:mc_heldout_ns} give the ratio of the Monte Carlo luminosity to that of the model, MC/model, so that a ratio above unity means that the model is too faint.
The ranges are over the bins $\Delta\mu = 0.1$ with at least 200 Monte Carlo packets, inside the funnel and beyond $\theta_{\rm c}$, without the correction and with it.
% source: transfer_correction_results.md sects. 2, 4, 5
% AUTHOR: the other source names (M51ULS, M81ULS, NGC1313H, NGC247S, NGC247SS, NGC300, NGC55, NGC6946, NGC7793)
%         are written as in the code; full names?

For the BH fits the corrected model gives MC/model = 0.94--1.03 inside the funnel and 0.78--1.23 beyond $\theta_{\rm c}$; without the correction the ratios are 0.67--2.5 inside, mostly with the model too faint, and 0.31--7.7 beyond.
In the bands 0.01--0.1, 0.1--1 and 1--10~keV the corrected ratios are 0.91--1.05 inside and 0.68--1.30 beyond; the worst case is NGC5408 BH1a at $i = 86^\circ$ in the 1--10~keV band.
The energy that reaches observers, $\int L_{\rm iso}\,{\rm d}\mu$ relative to the emitted energy, is 0.982--1.001 of that of the Monte Carlo.
We also compared the packet counts of the 86 BH calibration runs with those the model predicts.
A cell of this test is one run, one inclination group and one class of emitter temperature.
Inside the funnel each of the 20 bins in $\mu$ is a group; beyond $\theta_{\rm c}$ neighbouring bins are merged until a group spans at least 0.15 in $\mu$.
The temperature classes are bins of 0.25 in $\log T$ from 3.5 to 8.0.
A packet keeps the temperature of the element that emitted it, so the test is free of spectral noise: the spectrum is $\sum_j n_j B_\nu(T_j)$.
The model predicts $n_{\rm pred} = N L_{\rm cell}/L_{\rm tot}$ packets in a cell, where $L_{\rm cell}$ is the model luminosity into the cell, the direct escape plus $S_j h_j(\mu)$ summed over the emitting elements and integrated over the $\mu$ bins of the group, $L_{\rm tot}$ is the total luminosity of the run, and $N = 10^5$ is the number of packets in the run.
A cell with $n_{\rm pred} \ge 10$ is counted as off when
\begin{equation}
|n_{\rm MC} - n_{\rm pred}| > \max\left(0.1\,n_{\rm pred},\ 2\sqrt{n_{\rm pred}}\right) ,
\label{eq:mc_count_test}
\end{equation}
% code: ULXspectra-mc docs/scripts/mc_table/evaluate_counts.py (line 41, "bad = use & ..."), commit 8f2faa3;
%       definitions of cells, groups and n_pred as given by the author 2026-11-09
where $n_{\rm MC}$ is the number of Monte Carlo packets in the cell.
The second term is twice the Poisson noise of the expected count; the first is larger when $n_{\rm pred} > 400$.
Pooled over all runs, that is, the off cells of all runs divided by all tested cells of the region, 3.2\% of the cells inside the funnel and 21.2\% beyond $\theta_{\rm c}$ are off.
% source: transfer_correction_results.md sect. 4; commit 2272e8a; pooling: evaluate_counts.py (tally) and the author 2026-11-09

For the NS fits with the dipole surface and a deep throat (NGC5408 NS5, NS5a and NS6), most of the light comes from the central emitter: in NGC5408 NS5a 99\% of the emission, and 61\% of that from the throat ($x < 0.01$, $T$ up to 174 times $T(\rmag)$).
The walls of the throat face the axis, and the light reflected between them leaves near the axis.
With the BH classes alone these fits were at 0.64--2.25 inside the funnel and 0.17--0.82 beyond; with the dipole classes they are at 0.98--1.05 inside and 0.80--1.63 beyond.
The ten fits with the spherical emitter are at 0.95--1.00 inside and 0.66--1.48 beyond.
The energy that reaches observers is 1.001--1.011 of the emitted energy in the five fits checked.
% source: transfer_correction_results.md sect. 5; README "Visibility and spectrum" (summary of the NS check)

The correction has the following limits.
The BH table covers all the $\theta_{\rm c}$ nodes only for BH3a, the other BH presets at one node each, and BH2 was only checked, not calibrated; the NS central classes come from $\theta_{\rm c} = 45.6^\circ$ only.
Beyond $\theta_{\rm c}$ the model with the dipole surface is too faint by up to a factor of 1.6 (2.2 in the 0.1--10~keV band), and for the BH fits the error is largest at $i \gtrsim 55^\circ$.
Compton energy exchange and absorption in the wind and heating by the scattered light are left out both in the Monte Carlo and in the table, and the albedo of the NS body is unity by assumption.

\subsection{Collimation by the funnel: comparison with Ahlberg et al.}
\label{app:mc:funnel}
\label{app:mc:ahlberg_corrected}
% Merged 2026-11-19 (author): former subsections "Collimation by the funnel: comparison with Ahlberg et al." and
% "The corrected model against the funnel of Ahlberg et al."; result summarised in Sect. sec:disc:funnel.

We test the collimation of the light by the funnel in three steps against \textit{the opaque funnel} of \citet{Ahlberg2026}: 
\begin{itemize}
    \item[] our Monte Carlo of their opaque-funnel setting against their paper results (this step checks our Monte Carlo method);
    \item[]  the Monte Carlo through the `real' wind  against the opaque funnel, which shows the effect of the semi-transparent walls and the extended source;
    \item[]and the model with the transfer correction against both.
    But - comparison with \cite{Ahlberg2026} is carried out for different geometries. - needs to rewrite.
\end{itemize}

The result is summarised in Sect.~\ref{sec:disc:funnel}.

\paragraph{Their setup and our Monte Carlo of it.}
\citet{Ahlberg2026} computed the geometric beaming by a funnel in the shape of a conical frustum with infinitely optically thick, conservative walls and a point source at the centre of its base.
Their walls re-emit as Lambertian surfaces or reflect as an electron-scattering atmosphere; the two differ in the boost by at most 6\%.
% source: Ahlberg et al. 2026 (arXiv:2610.23089v1), Sect. 3.3 and Fig. 5 ("remains <~6% across the whole parameter space"); checked in the PDF 2026-11-10
They define the boost of the apparent luminosity $B(i)$ by $L_{\rm app}(i) = B(i) \int F\,{\rm d}\Omega$.
Averaged over $i \le \alpha$, where $\alpha$ is the half-opening angle of the funnel, its geometric limit is (their equation~7)
\begin{equation}
B_{\rm max} = \frac{1}{1 - \cos\alpha} ,
\label{eq:mc_Bmax}
\end{equation}
% source: Ahlberg et al. 2026 (arXiv:2610.23089v1), Eq. (7); checked in the PDF 2026-11-10
and the mean boost inside the funnel is approximated to about 10\% by (their equation~15)
\begin{equation}
\frac{\ln B}{\ln B_{\rm max}} \approx \exp\left(1.14\,\frac{\ln\sin\alpha}{\ln R}\right) ,
\label{eq:mc_AhlbergB}
\end{equation}
% source: Ahlberg et al. 2026 (arXiv:2610.23089v1), Eq. (15) and the sentence after it ("accuracy of ~10%"); checked in the PDF 2026-11-10
where $R$ is the distance from the centre of the base to the edge of the funnel in units of the base radius.
% source: docs/green_surface_monte_carlo.md, "Comparison with our MC"

We  reproduced their setup with a Monte Carlo of our own (\code{docs/scripts/funnel\_beaming\_mc.py}): a conical frustum with Lambertian walls, an isotropic point source, and the plane $z = 0$ as a mirror for the lower half of the funnel.

We  compared results of this setup, $2\times10^5$ packets per point, with the published results of \citet{Ahlberg2026}, read from their figures and \textbf{found a very good agreement which supports our MC transfer method} (see Fig.~\ref{fig:funnel_published}) .
% method: the figures of arXiv:2610.23089v1 are vector graphics; curves (Fig. 2) and grid cells (Fig. 4, colour -> value
%         through the viridis colorbar) are read from the drawing paths. Scripts and data: this repository,
%         figures/ahlberg2026/ (extract_fig2.py, extract_fig4.py, cone_mc_runs.py); numbers: figures/ahlberg2026/data/summary.txt.
%         Checks: their Fig. 2 curves integrate to 0.93-0.99 over mu (end at B = 0.01, the edge of the plot);
%         Fig. 4a grid reproduces their contour levels B = 4-20 within 0.05; resolution of Fig. 4a +-0.11 in B.
%         Code: ULXspectra branch mc-transfer-corrections, commit 2272e8a (docs/scripts/funnel_beaming_mc.py)
Their $B(i)$ is tallied in 500 bins of equal solid angle, $\Delta\mu = 0.002$.
% source: Ahlberg et al. 2026, Sect. 3.1 ("tallied them into 500 inclination bins of equal solid angle")
For Lambertian walls (their figure~2: $\alpha = 5^\circ$--$25^\circ$ at $R = 10^3$, and $\alpha = 25^\circ$ at $R = 10$--$10^5$), our mean boost over their bins at $i \le \alpha$ is 1.001--1.021 of theirs, and outside the funnel the median ratio of $B(i)$ over their bins is 0.990--1.017.
In all ten cases their curves match ours best if ours are moved by one bin ($\Delta\mu = 0.002$) towards the axis; with this shift the mean boosts at $i \le \alpha$ agree within 0.3\%.\footnote{There is no physical reason for the shift. Our comparison script tries shifting our curve by a few bins and finds that a shift of one bin ($\Delta\mu = 0.002$) fits their curves best. Even without the shift the mean boosts agree to 0--2\%, and with it to 0.3\%. The likely cause is how the bins are indexed or the steps drawn in their figure, but this is a guess.}
% interpretation (Claude): no physical reason; the shift is found by the fit in plot_ahlberg_comparison.py (summary.txt item 1)
% source: figures/ahlberg2026/data/summary.txt, items 1 (ratios 1.001-1.021 unshifted, 0.998-1.003 shifted; outside 0.990-1.017;

Their figure~4a gives the mean boost for the electron-scattering walls on a grid $\alpha = 6.25^\circ$--$45^\circ$, $R = 10$--$10^5$; our Lambertian mean boost is 0.94--1.02 of it at $\alpha = 10^\circ$--$45^\circ$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:funnel_published}c).

\subsection{Outlook: a slim disc}
\label{app:mc:slim}
% Source: ULXspectra-mc, branch mc-transfer-corrections, docs/slim_disc_geometry.md (commit 1896e10; PLAN C29, planned,
%         not implemented); sketch docs/scripts/plot_slim_disc_streamlines.py and figures (commit b2dc222);
%         MC estimate from the 43 runs of the C28 table with theta_c = 45.6 deg (same document, Sect. 4).
% Calculation-session notes 2026-11-21 and 2026-11-23 (author's request: beaming section of the appendix).
All fits use a flat disc ($\hd = 0$).
We considered, but did not implement, a slim disc, whose surface is
\begin{equation}
z_{\rm disc}(r) =
\begin{cases}
H_1 r, & r \le R_{\rm b},\\
H_1 R_{\rm b}, & R_{\rm b} < r \le (H_1/H_2) R_{\rm b},\\
H_2 r, & r > (H_1/H_2) R_{\rm b},
\end{cases}
\qquad R_{\rm b} = \max(\Rsph, \Rtrap),
\label{eq:slim_disc}
\end{equation}
% source: docs/slim_disc_geometry.md, Sect. 1 (commit 1896e10)
with $H_1 = 0.5$ and $H_2 = 0.05$ as proposed defaults: a conical bowl, a flat top, and the outer disc.
Inside $\Rsph$ the disc is critical and $z/r \approx$ const; outside it, in the radiation-pressure zone, the half-thickness $H \approx (3/8\pi)\,\kT \dot M_0/c$ is constant \citep{Shakura1973}; further out, where gas pressure dominates, $z/r \approx 0.01$--0.05.
The streamlines start on the bowl, with $\cos\theta_0$ linear in $r_0$ from $\cos\theta_{\rm c} = \omf$ at $\rin$ to $\cos\theta_{\rm d} = H_1/\sqrt{1 + H_1^2}$ at $\Rsph$, so that the outermost streamline is tangent to the bowl.
The funnel keeps its opening ($\theta_{\rm c} = 45.6^\circ$ for $\omf = 0.7$), and the wind fills $\theta_{\rm c}$--$\theta_{\rm d} = 45.6^\circ$--$63.4^\circ$ instead of $45.6^\circ$--$90^\circ$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:slim_disc}).

\textbf{The wind becomes denser but hardly more opaque vertically.}
For the NGC5408 BH3a fit, the peak density on a vertical rises by a factor 2.1--2.2, and at the same point in mid-wind by 3.4--5.1, but $\taup$ from the top to the disc rises only by a factor 1.03--1.15 (Fig.~\ref{fig:slim_disc_columns}).
The vertical column at a given $r$ is $\Sigma \approx \dot M_{\rm w}/(2\pi r v \sin\theta_0)$, which does not depend on the solid angle of the wind: a narrower wind is denser but also thinner in $z$.
The photosphere keeps its radial extent ($r = 84$--$8.3\times10^3\,\rin$) but is lifted onto the bowl, to $z \le 4.1\times10^3\,\rin$ (flat disc: $716\,\rin$).
The advection ratio $\taup v_{\rm w}/c$ changes by the same 12--15\%, and the energy budget is unchanged, so the wind temperature changes by about 10\% or less.
Above the flat top a gap without wind opens, of height $\approx H_1(r - R_{\rm b})$; light from the flat top at $i > \theta_{\rm d}$ escapes through it without meeting the wind.

\textbf{The beaming inside the funnel hardly changes.}
In the Monte Carlo of the 43 runs of the revised table with $\theta_{\rm c} = 45.6^\circ$, only 0--10\% (median 2\%) of the light emitted inside $\Rsph$ leaves at $i > 63.4^\circ$, whereas 51--97\% (median 73\%) of all the light already escapes inside the funnel.
Even if the bowl sent all the light it intercepts into the funnel, $L_{\rm iso}$ at $i < \theta_{\rm c}$ would rise by about 3\% (median) and at most 17\%.
The collimation is set by the wind funnel, not by the thickness of the disc.
The slim disc adds a second occultation edge at $\theta_{\rm d} = \arctan(1/H_1) = 63.4^\circ$: beyond it the bowl rim hides the centre and the inner wind, $L_{\rm iso}$ falls by about 25\% (median, up to 60\%), and the spectrum becomes softer.
% H_1 = 0.5: arctan(2) = 63.4 deg. Of the 182 best fits of the previous round, 13 have i > 63.4 deg (same document, Sect. 4).
Of the 182 best fits of the previous round, 13 have $i > 63.4^\circ$.
To implement this geometry, the table of the correction would need a new axis, $\theta_{\rm d}$, and the visibility would need to describe the gap.

The remaining mismatch beyond the funnel edge comes from the table of the correction, which sends too little scattered light beyond $\theta_{\rm c}$.
For the outer disc the deficit is 10--17\%, because the limit of an optically thin wind, in which the light is scattered in proportion to $\cos^2\theta_{\rm c}$, is not built into the table; for weak winds with a wide funnel it reaches a factor of 2--3.
Until the table is improved and the fits are redone, we take the ratios above as the systematic error of the model beyond the funnel edge.
